The platform

One planning layer on top of the systems you already run

No re-platform and no connector project. MetaFlow sits next to your ERP, reads its exports and hands your planners a plan they can check line by line.

Data in, without a project

Drop CSV exports or let your systems copy them to S3 or Azure. MetaFlow recognises each file, checks every row, and tells you in one sentence when a file looks different from last time.

A forecast for every store

Statistical methods and a machine-learning model compete on past data for each item, so short histories, stockouts and promotions don't throw the forecast off. Bring your own forecast if you have one.

One plan for the whole network

Supplier purchases and warehouse-to-store transfers are planned together, within case packs, minimums, storage, routes, ordering days and budgets, with fair shares when stock runs short.

Your planners stay in charge

Every line explains itself. Planners keep, change or skip it with a reason, try a what-if, and download one order file per supplier to place in their own system.

Why it matters

Most inventory networks are still planned in spreadsheets

Teams with a few warehouses and many stores decide every day or week what to buy and where to send it. The cost shows up twice: lost sales where stock ran out, and cash tied up where it sits unsold.

Rules go stale

Min/max levels set once a year ignore seasonality, promotions and the supplier who just slipped two weeks.

Stockouts hide demand

An empty shelf records zero sales. Next month's forecast drops, and the stockout repeats.

Locations planned alone

Warehouses and stores compete for the same stock with no view of the whole network.

Suites mean a re-platform

Enterprise planning suites take months to roll out, often cost six or seven figures a year and assume one ERP.

How it works

From your exports to a decided plan

  1. Drop your exports

    Stock, open orders, sales and items as CSV files, uploaded or copied to S3 or Azure.

  2. Checked before loading

    Each file is recognised and every row checked, with a fix for each issue.

  3. Forecast every store

    The best method per item wins on past data; your own forecast can override it.

  4. Plan the network

    Purchases and transfers together, within your real operating limits.

  5. The planner decides

    Start with what needs attention, try a what-if, keep, change or skip each line.

  6. Learn from actuals

    Results compare forecasts and plans with what really sold.

Drop your exports

Drop up to ten CSV files at once. MetaFlow works out what each one is, checks every row and shows what it read before anything loads.

  • Confirm a file's reading once; the same export loads by itself next time
  • Uploads and files arriving in S3 or Azure follow the same path
  • Templates with sample data for anything you don't export yet

Start with what needs attention

The plan opens on what matters: items that run out before the next delivery, lines cut by a limit, overstock and what changed since the last review.

  • Urgency, order date, arrival and days of cover on every line
  • Keep, change or skip in one click, with a reason
  • Re-plans itself when fresh stock or open orders arrive

Every line answers the planner's questions

Planners don't trust numbers they can't check, so every recommendation shows its math and its reason.

  • Why this quantity: forecast over the lead time, stock on hand and on order, the service target
  • Why it was cut: the specific limit that bound it
  • What changed since the last review, with decisions kept on unchanged lines

Test a change before you commit

Try higher demand, a late supplier, a tighter budget or a different service target, and compare it with the plan side by side.

  • Order value, projected stockouts, ending stock and lines changed
  • A what-if never changes the real plan or its order files
