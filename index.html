The problem

Networks are still planned in spreadsheets

Retailers and distributors with a few warehouses and many stores decide every day or every week what to buy and where to send it. Most still do it with static min/max rules and spreadsheets. The cost shows up twice: lost sales where stock ran out, and cash tied up where it sits unsold.

1

Rules go stale

Min/max levels set once a year ignore seasonality, promotions and the supplier who just slipped two weeks.

2

Stockouts hide demand

When the shelf is empty, sales read as zero. Next month's forecast drops, and the stockout repeats.

3

Locations planned alone

Distribution centers and stores compete for the same stock with no view of the whole network.

4

Suites mean a re-platform

Enterprise planning suites take months to roll out, often cost six or seven figures a year and assume one ERP. Most teams' data lives in several systems.

How it works

From your exports to a decided plan

Getting data in is the hardest part of planning software, so MetaFlow starts from the files teams already produce. No connector project, no new system of record.

01 Drop your exports Stock, open orders, sales and products as CSV files, uploaded or copied to S3 or Azure.
02 Checked before loading Every row is validated with a fix for each issue. Files that look different from last time are flagged in one plain sentence.
03 Forecast every store Statistical methods and a machine-learning model compete on past data; the best one per item wins.
04 Plan the whole network Supplier purchases and store transfers together, within case packs, minimums, storage and budgets.
05 The planner decides Review what needs attention, try a what-if, keep, change or skip each line, download order files.
06 Learn from actuals A results page compares forecasts and plans with what really sold, cycle after cycle.
The product today

A small product that does the planning job end to end

MetaFlow is deliberately lean: a few screens a planner uses every day, built on a careful planning engine and a multi-tenant SaaS foundation.

Data

Bring in what you already export, and confirm how MetaFlow reads it once.

  • CSV / Excel upload, or files copied to Amazon S3 or Azure Blob
  • Templates with sample data and plain definitions for every dataset
  • Row-by-row checks with a fix for each issue
  • One "Refresh and plan" button

Demand forecast

Demand for every carried product at every store, from the register, not the warehouse.

  • Statistical methods and LightGBM compete per item
  • Stockout periods treated as a floor, not as zero demand
  • Promotion periods kept out of the baseline
  • Bring your own forecast, or adjust any day or week and re-plan

Replenishment recommendations

One coordinated plan of purchases and transfers, with order-by dates, daily or weekly.

  • Distribution center and store targets set together
  • Fair-share allocation when stock runs short
  • Case packs, minimums, storage, routes, calendars and budgets
  • Re-plans when fresh stock or open orders arrive

Explanations, not a black box

Every line answers the planner's questions

Planners don't trust numbers they can't check. MetaFlow shows the math and the reason behind each recommendation, from the data the customer provided.

Why this quantity? The forecast over the lead time, stock on hand and on order, the service target, and the case-pack rounding that produced the number.
Why was this line cut? The specific limit that bound it: warehouse storage, a supplier minimum, a closed ordering day, a budget or a route that isn't allowed.
What changed since I last looked? Lines that moved after fresh stock or open orders were loaded are tagged, and decisions on unchanged lines carry over.
What if things go wrong? A stock projection for the plan, plus what-ifs: demand up 20%, a supplier two weeks late, a tighter budget, a different service target.
Built for an IT review

Safe to put next to the system of record

MetaFlow is a planning layer, not another system to keep in sync. It reads files and recommends; the customer's ERP stays in charge of transactions.

Read-only by design Read-only storage credentials, encrypted at rest. MetaFlow never changes customer files or writes back.
Multi-tenant from day one Every query is scoped to the customer's workspace, with automated tests on isolation.
Roles and audit Role-based access, invitations limited to company domains, and a full audit log of who did what.
Humans decide No automatic orders, reservations or approvals. Every decision is recorded with its reason.
